How to Use the Generative Design Setup

Welcome to the PCLS problem definition webapp. This tool allows you to visually construct regions, set analysis parameters, and export your problem space directly into a JSON schema ready for the custom topology optimiser.

1. Object Types & Roles

  • Preserve Regions (Green): Solid areas of material that MUST remain in the final design. These act as connection points where boundary conditions (loads and constraints) are applied.
  • Obstacle Regions (Red): Void areas (keep-outs) where material is strictly forbidden from growing during optimisation to avoid interference.
  • Primitives (Shapes): Base geometries (cuboids and cylinders) that build up the regions. Primitives themselves only represent mathematical bounding volumes; their combined CSG operation is what ultimately forms the solid region.

2. Operations & Hierarchy

  • Adding/Subtracting: Select a region, choose a shape (Cuboid/Cylinder) and an operation (+ Add or - Subtract), then click "+ Add Shape". Subtracting a primitive will carve out geometry from the collective region.
  • Grouping: Tick the checkboxes next to two or more independent regions and click "Link & Group". Grouping merges their physical identities, meaning boundary conditions applied to the group apply collectively to all regions within it simultaneously.
  • Duplicating (Dup): You can duplicate entire regions (spawning exact copies at identical physical spaces) or individual primitives (which applies a small physical offset for convenience).

3. Colors & Widgets Glossary

  • Green Translucent Volumes: Preserve (Solid) Regions.
  • Red Translucent Volumes: Obstacle (Void) Regions.
  • Orange Wireframes: Additive (+) Primitives.
  • Red Wireframes: Subtractive (-) Primitives.
  • Blue Highlights / Padlock Widget: Highlighted faces represent constrained areas. A padlock widget hovers in 3D space to indicate applied constraints. An opaque, locked padlock means Encastre (all 6 DOFs fixed). A slightly translucent, open padlock means only partial DOFs are fixed.
  • Orange Highlights / Arrow Widgets: Highlighted faces represent loaded areas. Straight red arrows represent Force vectors, pointing towards the geometric centroid. Curved purple arrows with a central axis line denote Moment loads.

4. Movement & Camera

  • Camera: Left-click and drag to rotate the view. Right-click and drag to pan. Scroll to zoom dynamically directly toward your mouse cursor.
  • Transform: Select a Region or Shape to reveal the movement gizmo. Use the toggle buttons (Move/Rotate/Scale) or enter specific values into the Properties panel on the left.

5. Boundary Conditions (Loads & Constraints)

  • Applying Constraints: Select a Preserve region or group. You can check Encastre Constraint to fix all 6 degrees of freedom automatically, or expand the DOFs ▾ dropdown to pick exactly which of the 6 degrees of freedom (Tx, Ty, Tz, Rx, Ry, Rz) to constrain. Choose to apply it to the bulk volume, or select specific faces by clicking them in the viewport (highlights blue).
  • Applying Loads: Click "+ Add Load" to add Force or Moment loads. Like constraints, choose whether the load applies to the bulk volume or specific faces (highlights orange).

6. Exporting & Importing

  • Fill out the Analysis and Optimiser tabs according to your required simulation constraints.
  • Click Validate & Export JSON. The tool will rigorously validate your physics setup—ensuring that all applied loads are resisted by corresponding constraints and that global rigid body translation is completely prevented—before allowing the `.json` file to export.
  • You can reload your work anytime via the Import JSON button.
